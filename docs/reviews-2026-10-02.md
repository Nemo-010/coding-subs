# Deep reviews — 2026-10-02 pass

Five reviews of this pass, each with a single question, the evidence it was tested against, and a
verdict. Where a review found a defect, the fix is recorded rather than repaired silently.

---

## Review 1 — Is "BEST DEAL" answerable from anything in this repo?

**Question.** The 2026-09-20 pass named one plan the best deal and was challenged for it. This pass
names five different "best" rows. Is that a dodge, or is the question genuinely underdetermined?

**Test.** Take each candidate and ask what evidence class supports it. GLM Lite's ceiling comes from
a first-party credit table. SuperGrok's 190× comes from a meter someone else ticked on a different
workload. OpenCode Go's $0.0013/M comes from a first-party per-model ceiling grid whose *token price*
is published but whose *usage pattern* is assumed. These are three different quantities: a ceiling,
a measured worth, and a price.

**Verdict: the question is underdetermined, and saying so is the finding.** A ceiling is not a
usage; a measured multiplier is not a ceiling; a grid price is neither. The five-row answer is not
a hedge — it is the honest decomposition. The pass is, however, *required* to state which class each
row is, and it does, in the "Evidence class" column. **Accepted, with the obligation to keep the
classes visible in every future pass.**

## Review 2 — Does the GLM number survive its own fine print?

**Question.** GLM Lite is quoted here at up to 1,264M tokens/month. Z.ai's own table produces that
number only at a 95% cache-hit rate and only at the off-peak credit rate. Is the headline figure
doing hidden work?

**Test.** Re-read the two sources. (a) The estimated-token table gives Lite at 95% cache:
GLM-5.3 48–97M/week; GLM-5.3-Flash 146–292M/week. Multiply by 4.33 weeks → 208–420M and 632–1,264M
per month. The *range* is peak-to-off-peak, not uncertainty — Z.ai says so in "How the Range Is
Calculated". (b) The all-day off-peak window runs **2026-09-25 to 2026-10-07**. Today is 2026-10-02.
So the maximum column is currently the default, and it stops being the default **five days from
now**.

**Verdict: the number is correct today and expires.** This is exactly the failure mode the
2026-09-13 pass warned about with the GLM Flash campaign date. The fix applied: the pass states the
campaign end date (2026-10-07) next to the figure, and lists its withdrawal first under "What would
falsify this pass". **Accepted with an expiry label. A future reader who sees 1,264M without the
date has been misled by us, not by Z.ai.**

## Review 3 — The metered multipliers: is adopting someone else's measurement legitimate here?

**Question.** This repository's whole method is "first-party pages fetched and archived on the
research date". The headline table of this pass is a third party's meter study. Is that a
methodological collapse?

**Test.** Read the meter study's own method and caveats. It ticks the vendor's own meter — not a
scraped page — prices every call at the vendor's *public API list*, publishes floor/ceiling brackets
per step, and states plainly that it ran one workload, once, on four plans. It also publishes the
cases where its own numbers *do not* hold constant (Grok's step brackets fail to intersect; Codex's
spread 9.7–10.7×).

**Verdict: legitimate, if and only if it is labelled.** It is added to
`data/subscription-multipliers.csv` as its own evidence class, with the third-party URL in every
row, the measured date, and the uncertainty the study itself published. It is not merged into
"VERIFIED". The stronger point is *why* this matters: a meter is a stronger instrument than a
marketing table, and a repo that refuses to cite instruments it did not hold is refusing the
strongest evidence in the field on a technicality. **Accepted; the evidence-class vocabulary is
extended from two classes to four (VERIFIED / DOCUMENTED / MEASURED / THIRD-PARTY).**

## Review 4 — Free tiers: does the index repeat numbers it cannot source?

**Question.** The 2026-09-20 pass advertised "222 providers" from models.dev without ever checking
whether models.dev *prices* are first-party. This pass adds 34 free surfaces. Same trap?

**Test.** Walk each row in `data/free-tier-index.csv` back to a source and ask: does that source
state the number? Failures found and fixed: (a) GitHub Copilot Free was quoted on an earlier pass
at 2,000 completions + 50 chat requests; the dated 2026-09-28 read of Cursor's own pricing page and
the 2026-08-03 Copilot reading disagree on what the *current* Free column says, and the sources say
the old pair is obsolete. The row now records the allowance as "an allowance of GitHub AI Credits"
with the obsolete pair named as obsolete rather than quoted as current. (b) Ollama Cloud's free
"starter allowance" has never had a published amount *or* a published starter-model list; the row
says so instead of carrying a number. (c) Onomeo's check-in credits are real but capped on premium
models at 50,000/day, about **12 gpt-6-astra calls** — the cap, not the credit balance, is the
usable figure, and both are in the row.

**Verdict: accepted, with one deliberate omission.** Navarino-style "free tier" claims from
aggregator marketing pages were not carried into the index at all: a free model list is a *list*,
not a *limit*, and this repo indexes limits. Rows without a dated source and a stated figure are
labelled `unrated`/`unpublished`, not estimated.

## Review 5 — Does the pass actually integrate the two issues, or gesture at them?

**Question.** Issues #1 and #2 were open in upstream. This pass claims to have integrated them. Is
that a real integration or a section heading?

**Test.** Trace an artefact from each issue to a committed file. Issue #1 (four repos): each of the
four was fetched and committed under `sources/thirdparty-*`. One of them
(`real-api-pricing`) is the direct source of **11 rows** in `data/cost-per-usable-token.csv` and of
the Claude/ChatGPT/MiniMax comparisons in the report; another (`verified-ai-free-tiers`) is the
source of **14 rows** in `data/free-tier-index.csv`; a third (`awesome-free-llm-api`) is the source
of **11** more; the fourth (`agentdeals`) is the source of the dated-verification column *design*
and of 6 rows. Issue #2 (two repos): `phuryn/experiments` is the source of all 9 rows of
`data/subscription-multipliers.csv` and of the pass's central correction to the 09-20 ranking;
`real-api-pricing` is used for the saturated-use figures. Every adopted number carries the
third-party URL and the measured date.

**Verdict: integrated, with a stated ceiling on the claim.** The pass did **not** re-run the meter
study, and it says so. It also did not adopt the suggested repos' *rankings* — only their dated
numbers and their discipline. And it declined to adopt anything that could not be traced to a
dated read, which is why the four repos produced 42 adopted rows and not a copied list.

## Summary of what this review pass did not establish

- It did not measure any meter itself. Every MEASURED row is someone else's instrument.
- It did not verify Z.ai's cache-hit assumption; the 95% column is Z.ai's, not ours.
- It did not price the Kimi new-membership ladder (behind a JS paywall) or the Z.ai Team seat.
- It did not measure availability, latency or throughput for any plan or free tier.
- It did not resolve the Command Code direction of travel: the 2026-10-02 page shows *smaller*
  effective-usage deals than 2026-09-20 for Go and a *restated* range for GOAT, which is a
  repricing downward, but a single pair of snapshots cannot distinguish a trend from a page edit.
